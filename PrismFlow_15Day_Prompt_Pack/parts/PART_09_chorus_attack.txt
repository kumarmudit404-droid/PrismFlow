BASELINE ATTACK (baseline_attacks.py)
  Standard PGD applied to each compromised view INDEPENDENTLY. This is what the
  existing literature does and it is the comparison point.

CHORUS ATTACK (chorus.py)
  Select k compromised views out of n. Optimise their perturbations JOINTLY:

    maximise over {delta_v : v in C}
        sum_{v in C} log b_v[target_class]
        - beta * sum_{i,j in C} || b_i - b_j ||^2
    subject to  || delta_v ||_inf <= epsilon

  The second term is the entire idea. It penalises DISAGREEMENT among the
  attacker's own views. Low mutual disagreement keeps Dempster conflict mass C
  low, which collapses the vacuity product u1*u2/(1-C) and inflates belief in the
  wrong class. The attack does not evade the trust mechanism; it operates it.

  Configurable: k in {1, 2, 3}, epsilon sweep, beta sweep, PGD steps, target
  class selection strategy (least-likely / random / fixed).

MEASURE for three systems - naive fusion, PrismFlow without discount,
PrismFlow with discount:
  - attack success rate
  - mean confidence in the WRONG class when the attack succeeds
  - vacuity under attack
  - mean pairwise dependence among compromised views
  - ENIV under attack
  - ECE under attack

ALSO REPORT - this is the headline comparison
  Mean pairwise correlation rho_bar under attack versus under clean input.
  If rho_bar barely moves while the attack succeeds, that is itself a finding:
  it shows the standard average-case dependence measure is blind to the attack,
  and it directly motivates the tail-dependence work in Part 13. Record the
  numbers either way.

TESTS
  - the attack objective decreases monotonically over PGD iterations
  - perturbations respect the epsilon constraint
  - k=0 leaves predictions bit-identical
